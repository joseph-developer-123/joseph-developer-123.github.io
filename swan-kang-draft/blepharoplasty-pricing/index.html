How Much Does Blepharoplasty Cost in the UK?

Eyelid surgery removes excess skin, muscle and fat around the eyes to restore a more rested, refreshed appearance, and in some cases to improve vision where heavy skin is blocking the visual field. Across the UK, prices typically range from around £2,000 for simple procedures to £11,000 or more for complex, combined surgery, depending on whether the upper eyelids, lower eyelids, or both are being treated, along with the surgeon's experience and the clinic itself.

Below is a clear breakdown of Dr Swan Kang's fees for upper, lower and combined blepharoplasty, along with the factors that affect price and what should be included in any quote you receive.

When considering eyelid surgery, your eye health should always come first.

Blepharoplasty is not simply about removing excess skin. The eyelids play an important role in protecting the eye, maintaining the tear film and preserving healthy vision. A careful assessment of your eyes, eyelid anatomy and facial structure is therefore essential before deciding whether surgery is appropriate and what procedure is best suited to you.

Dr Kang's approach is to achieve a natural, balanced result while prioritising the health, function and safety of your eyes.

Upper Blepharoplasty

Upper eyelid surgery treats loose, hooded skin that rests heavily on the lashes, which can create a tired appearance and, in more advanced cases, obstruct the upper visual field. The procedure is usually performed under local or twilight anaesthetic, with incisions placed carefully within the natural eyelid crease to keep scarring discreet.

Lower Blepharoplasty

Lower eyelid surgery addresses under-eye bags, prolapsed fat pads, skin laxity and hollowing in the tear trough area. The lower lid anatomy is more delicate than the upper lid, requiring precise technique to protect both the appearance and function of the eye. This is reflected in the higher fee compared with upper lid surgery.

Combined Upper and Lower Eyelid Surgery

Treating both the upper and lower eyelids in a single procedure is a common choice for patients wanting balanced, all-round rejuvenation. Combining the two typically works out more cost-effective than two separate operations, as surgeon, theatre and anaesthesia fees are shared across one recovery period.

What Affects the Cost of Blepharoplasty?

Prices vary between clinics and surgeons for several reasons:

  • Surgeon expertise — a Consultant Oculoplastic Surgeon has years of dedicated training in eyelid and orbital anatomy, reflected in their fee.
  • Type of anaesthesia — local anaesthetic is generally more affordable than twilight sedation or general anaesthetic, which involve additional specialist and facility costs.
  • Complexity of the case — revision surgery, or cases requiring more extensive fat repositioning, takes more time and skill than a straightforward procedure.
  • Clinic location and facilities — the standard of the clinical environment and aftercare provided can affect overall cost.

What Should Be Included in Your Quote?

A transparent quote should leave no hidden surprises. Before booking, check that your quote includes:

  • Your pre-operative consultation and assessment
  • The surgeon's fee
  • Anaesthetist and hospital or theatre fees
  • Post-operative follow-up appointments and suture removal

Always ask for a full written breakdown at your initial consultation.

Is Blepharoplasty Available on the NHS?

The NHS does not fund eyelid surgery for purely cosmetic reasons. Funding may be available where there is a clear medical need, such as:

  • Severe eyelid drooping (ptosis) that significantly blocks the visual field
  • Visual field test results from an optician confirming major obstruction
  • Chronic eye irritation or infection caused by excess eyelid skin

Even where these criteria are met, waiting times for non-urgent NHS procedures can be lengthy, which is why many patients choose to be assessed privately.
