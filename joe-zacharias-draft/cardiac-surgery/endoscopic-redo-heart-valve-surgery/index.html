Endoscopic Redo Heart Valve Surgery

An overview of minimally invasive approaches for patients presenting with heart valve deterioration, avoiding a second sternotomy under the care of Mr Joseph Zacharias.

Identifying Valve Deterioration

Over the past 20 years, a traditional median sternotomy has been the most common surgical approach for initial heart valve procedures. However, valves can deteriorate over time. Typically, this deterioration is identified during a routine echocardiogram when patients present with symptoms such as:

  • Shortness of breath
  • Unexplained tiredness or fatigue
  • Swelling of the legs and ankles

When these signs appear, a redo operation is often required to repair or replace the failing valve and restore cardiac function.

The Minimally Invasive Redo Approach

Avoiding the structural trauma of dividing the sternum for a second operation.

An endoscopic, minimally invasive (small surgical incision as shown to the left of the picture above) approach can be highly appropriate for redo cardiac surgery. This advanced keyhole technique accesses the heart through the side of the chest, entirely avoiding the need to divide the sternum for a second time.


Mr Zacharias and his team have one of the largest experiences of redo endoscopic heart valve surgery in the UK. Their highly successful outcomes and specialized surgical techniques have been widely recognised and presented at major national and international medical meetings.

Consultation and Next Steps

If you have previously had heart surgery and are now experiencing symptoms of valve deterioration, an endoscopic approach could offer a significantly less invasive route to recovery.


If you would like to be considered for this approach for redo surgery, please contact us and we will be happy to advise you further and arrange a comprehensive clinical assessment.
